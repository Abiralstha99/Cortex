Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants convert light energy into chemical energy.
Chlorophyll absorbs light, primarily in the blue and red wavelengths.
The light-dependent reactions occur in the thylakoid membranes and produce ATP and NADPH.
The Calvin cycle fixes carbon dioxide into sugars in the stroma of the chloroplast.
Water is split during photolysis, releasing oxygen as a byproduct.
Stomata regulate gas exchange and transpiration in leaves.
Guard cells open and close stomata in response to light, CO2, and water status.
Cellular respiration breaks down glucose to release energy as ATP in mitochondria.
Glycolysis occurs in the cytoplasm and does not require oxygen.
The Krebs cycle oxidizes acetyl-CoA and feeds electrons into the electron transport chain.
